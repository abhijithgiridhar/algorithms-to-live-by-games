Variant 3: Waiting has a cost

The classic problem treats searching as free — the only cost of holding out is running out of options. Here, every week you wait costs you something real, so the goal changes: you're not trying to land the single best offer, you're trying to maximize what you actually keep once the cost of waiting is subtracted.

The fix is a threshold — a "good enough" price, set once at the start. Take the first offer that clears it. Surprisingly, that price never drops: if the costs and the range of offers don't change, tomorrow looks exactly like today, so there's no reason to lower your standard as time passes.

  1. Estimate the range of offers you expect (lowest to highest).
  2. Estimate what each extra week of waiting costs you.
  3. Set a threshold: the more waiting costs, the lower it should be.
  4. Accept the first offer at or above the threshold — every week, the same threshold.

The formula (offers spread evenly across a range): threshold = highest offer − √(2 × cost per week × size of range).
