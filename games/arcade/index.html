Chapter 2: Explore / Exploit

Every one of these three games was secretly worth a different number of points per hit — and that multiplier was reshuffled for every single player. You had no way to know which was best except by actually playing all three.

This is exactly the dilemma computer scientists call the multi-armed bandit problem: you have several options with unknown payoffs, limited time, and every minute spent testing one option is a minute not spent cashing in on whatever you already know works. Spend too long exploring, and you waste your remaining time on games you already knew weren't great. Commit too early, and you might be grinding the worst one the whole time.

  1. Explore early — try all three games briefly to get a feel for which one is paying out best.
  2. Exploit once you're fairly confident — spend most of your remaining time on your current best guess.
  3. The less time you have left, the less exploring makes sense — a bad decision now has less time to be corrected.

Your regret, quantified

Computer scientists call the gap between what you actually scored and what you could have scored (if you'd known the best game from second one) your regret. You can't get it to zero — some exploring is always necessary — but a good strategy keeps it small.

Where you see it: a streaming app deciding whether to recommend your favourite genre again or try something new. A doctor choosing a well-known treatment vs. a promising new one. A restaurant-goer deciding between their usual order and today's special.
